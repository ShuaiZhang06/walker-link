Where every part of this submission came from, who owns it, and under what
terms. Covers both the game and the walkthrough film.

**Short version:** the engine architecture and the original level are the
starter author's; the character drawing and Section 03 are mine; every visual
asset in the game is original vector drawing in GDScript; nothing was purchased,
nothing was generated by an image, video or music model, and no third-party
sprite, texture, font file or audio clip is redistributed in this repository.

## 2 · Game art and audio

**All art is original vector drawing**, written as GDScript `draw_rect`,
`draw_line`, `draw_colored_polygon` and `draw_string` calls in
`features/player/player.gd::_draw()` and `game/session.gd::_draw()`. There is no
sprite sheet, no imported image, no texture file, no shader, and no atlas in
this repository. The character, the terrain, the spikes, the flag, the hills and
the HUD are all geometry evaluated at run time.

**The game has no audio at all** — no music, no effects, no UI sound. Nothing was
licensed, recorded or generated.

### The Link homage

The player character is a **fan-styled homage** to the visual language of
Nintendo's Link — a green pointed cap and a sword-and-shield pose — **drawn from
scratch as geometry by me**. No Nintendo asset, sprite, model, sound or font is
used, copied, traced or redistributed. *The Legend of Zelda* and *Link* are
trademarks of Nintendo; this is unpublished coursework, not a distributed
product, and the in-game character is named for this project
(`WALKER / LINK`) rather than presented as Nintendo's character. If this were
ever to be released rather than submitted, the character would be redrawn.

### The recovered collection

`GAME-BRIEF.md` refers to a separate `jumping-man-godot` recovery collection
(115 art files, 11 Unity C# scripts) that the starter author holds locally.
**None of it is included in this repository and none of it was used.** Its
attribution and redistribution rights were never established, which is exactly
why the build uses original placeholder geometry instead.

## 6 · What the AI contributed

Claude (Opus 5, via Claude Code). Everything below was implemented by the AI
under an authorisation I gave step by step; nothing was merged that I had not
read. The game-side split is also recorded, in more detail and in the order it
happened, in [FRICTIONAL.md §4](FRICTIONAL.md).

**The game.** Locating the drawing, collider, level data, camera clamp, finish
logic and tests in the starter; deriving the reach budget from `tuning.gd`;
every GDScript edit — `player.gd::_draw()`, the `session.gd` `_draw()`
parameterisation, the Section 03 level JSON, the route fixture, the nine new
checks; the measurement fixtures and take-off sweeps that produced **every
number quoted in this repository**; `tests/capture_character.gd` and
`tests/capture_level.gd`; running the headless suites. Also the first drafts of
`CHANGE-BRIEF.md`, `TEST-REPORT.md`, the README changes and the commit messages.

**The beat sheet.** The 18-beat structure and act order; choosing which of the
seven captures and which frame ranges fill each slot; the replay/held-frame
recipe in `shot.capture_plan`; the `qc.full_bleed` and `qc.contrast_regions`
declarations and their written reasons; `coverage.json` — mapping 18 implemented
features to a capture, a beat and a timecode, and listing the 6 GDD features
that are not built, with reasons.

**The script.** Every word of `narration_text`, written against the measured
numbers rather than around them, then traced claim by claim in `FACTCHECK.md`.
Also the on-screen card copy, the two prompts, the Chinese facing translation in
`SCRIPT.md`, and `CAPTURE.md` / `RIFF.md` / `SHOTLIST.md` / `PROMPTS.md` /
`_qc/WALKTHROUGH-REVIEW.md`.

**The visuals.** No image, video or animation in the film was generated by a
model. The AI wrote the capture harness (`capture_walkthrough.gd`), drove the
seven takes through real engine input, transcoded and cut every clip, and
composited the provenance chip, the replay labels and the held-frame labels. The
non-gameplay cards are existing toolkit Remotion components (§4) given props;
the AI chose the components, wrote their content, and replaced one
(`ClaudeWindow` → `ClaudeCodeBeat`) when the first measured 29 % of the safe
area and set the code too small to read. **Everything you see of the game
itself is the game** — the starter's rendering plus my `_draw()` changes,
photographed, not re-created.

**The narration.** Local Kokoro `am_onyx` reading `narration_text` verbatim. It
is a named preset voice introduced on screen as "Liam, in for Bear" — not a
clone of anybody, not an impression, and not a claim that a person spoke it.

**Its own errors, kept.** The AI produced the predictions in `CHANGE-BRIEF.md`
§4 that measurement later overturned — F8, and the ~11 px take-off window that
measured 24 px. Those are logged rather than edited away. It also shipped two
timing defects into the first cut (a six-decimal rounding that added a frame to
three beats, and a container duration that triggered a 1.000028 retime); it
found and fixed both, and they are written up in `_qc/WALKTHROUGH-REVIEW.md`.

## 7 · What I decided, checked, changed and rejected

*Shuai Zhang. I can explain every line of this change, including the parts I did
not type.*

**Two kinds of decision are listed below, and they are not the same.** The
game-side ones I made as the work happened, one approval at a time — the record
is [FRICTIONAL.md §4](FRICTIONAL.md) and the revision log in
[CHANGE-BRIEF.md §6](CHANGE-BRIEF.md). The film-side ones, marked **(film)**,
were taken by the AI during the build while following the
`godot-waikthrough` skill's rules, reported to me, and **ratified by me on
review** — I watched the export and accepted them. They are listed here because
I stand behind them, not because I typed them first.

**Decided.**
- To fork the starter rather than begin again, and to keep `upstream` read-only.
- The character concept — a Link-styled adventurer — and its readability rules:
  the body must stay inside the 18 × 28 collider, props may overhang by at most
  ~5 px in a secondary colour and never get a collision shape, everything
  mirrors on `facing`, and the airborne pose must differ from standing.
- The hard constraint that `features/player/tuning.gd` and the collider are
  untouchable: if a jump turns out to be impossible, the geometry moves, not
  the tuning.
- Section 03's intent: a step to be jumped *over*, one stone, one chasm.
- The working method: plan first, one step per approval, a commit per step.
- **(film)** The `walker` framing, what goes in the verdict, and the honesty
  boundary — label the scripted input on screen, never fake a completion,
  never change the game to hide a defect.
- That the MP4 stays out of GitHub and is linked from the README by name and
  SHA-256 instead.

**Checked.**
- Played the build by hand with the normal launcher and the normal keyboard —
  every revision below came from playing it, not from reading diffs.
- Inspected the rendered pose plates and level plates, which is where three
  character defects surfaced: the cap tail covering the ear, the sword
  invisible against the light backdrop, and an airborne pose identical to
  standing.
- Caught the `Machine` row in `TEST-REPORT.md` still describing the starter
  author's machine rather than mine.
- Read the documents back in translation to confirm they said what I meant.
- Watched the finished 4K export end to end.

**Changed.**
- Section 03, twice: the "spike stair" that was really walkable raised floor
  became a short block to hop; then the two-route fork became a single stone.
- The step height (40 px, against the 36 px suggested), the number of pits
  (one), both flat stretches shortened — which shrank the world from the
  planned 1600 to 1480 px.
- The flat blade became a five-point tapered polygon, after playing.
- The hill row: the starter's three restored exactly, one whole hill at the
  finish.
- The in-game title, `WALKER / JUMPMAN` → `WALKER / LINK`, and the menu copy to
  describe the real route.
- **(film)** Cut B03 before an unrelated death card it never mentions, cut B09
  before the completion so it belongs to B13, and split a 36-second static
  verdict card in two.

**Rejected.**
- The two-route fork in Section 03, after the sweep measured 5 landings on the
  high ledge and **0** on the low stone. `CHANGE-BRIEF.md` §2's
  "precision-at-height versus precision-at-distance" is withdrawn in writing
  rather than quietly dropped.
- The even-320 hill spacing, even though it fixed a real overlap, because it
  moved two of the starter's own hills. Correctness was not the point;
  preserving the starter's picture was.
- Raising `jump_velocity` to make a jump reachable.
- "Fixing" the route test by trimming its expectation — the completion
  assertion got *stricter* instead.
- **(film)** Substituting another brand's audio for the missing outro jingle —
  it ships silent and the blocker is reported instead; re-shooting the chasm
  take to match the prediction in CHANGE-BRIEF, so the take that actually
  happened is the take in the film; and lowering the strict frame gate to
  obtain a pass.

**Deferred, and it cost me.** Claude flagged the F8 overlap risk before
implementation and I chose to defer it. It later forced the Section 03 rebuild.
